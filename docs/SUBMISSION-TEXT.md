# Space Apps Challenge 2026 — Project Submission Text

## Project Title
**SOL COMMANDER — Mars: Last Habitat**

## Short Description (Elevator Pitch)
An educational 3D Mars exploration and decision game for kids aged 7–12. Step onto the surface of Mars as Junior Commander Nova, navigate a realistic low-gravity habitat with your faithful rover Rusty, and make evidence-based decisions across 88 Martian Sols to keep your crew safe through dust storms, cold greenhouse nights, and solar particle events.

---

## Detailed Project Description

### The Challenge & Educational Goal
Humanity's journey to Mars requires solving real life-support and planetary science challenges: thin carbon dioxide air, extreme diurnal thermal swings, airborne dust blocking solar arrays, perchlorates in Martian soil, and solar radiation events.

Too often, educational space games either oversimplify these concepts or make emergencies frightening and arbitrary. **Sol Commander** was created to teach children the astronaut mindset:
> **"Look. Listen. Think. Then decide."**

Every emergency in the game stems from an underlying physical cause that the player observes through early warning signs and daily chores before a decision is required.

### Gameplay & Mechanics
1. **Act 1: Settling In (Sol 1)**:
   - Learn low-gravity movement ($3.73\text{ m/s}^2$ gravity vs $9.81\text{ m/s}^2$ Earth) by catching glowing gravity crystals.
   - Meet the specialist crew through daily base chores: Rafi in the Bio-Dome greenhouse, Kenji at the solar array, and Dr. Amara and Lucía in the Habitat Core.
   - Inspect Oxygen Tank 2 and notice a faint tick-hiss sound (foreshadowing the Sol 21 leak).
   - Write the sunset Commander's Log and sleep as Sol 2–20 postcards elapse.

2. **Five Cause-and-Effect Missions**:
   - **Mission 1 (Sol 21) — Whisper in Tank 2**: Thermal diurnal expansion loosens a valve seal; player traces the 3D acoustic hiss to isolate the pipe with a shut-off valve.
   - **Mission 2 (Sol 34) — Red Sky**: Atmospheric dust blocks solar irradiance; player learns how dust dims solar cells and prioritizes life-support batteries.
   - **Mission 3 (Sol 52) — Water You Can't Drink Yet**: Recycler filter maintenance and ground-penetrating radar scanning for buried ice containing salty chemicals (perchlorates).
   - **Mission 4 (Sol 67) — Cold Greenhouse**: Outside temperatures drop to $-63^\circ\text{C}$; player balances heating and water to protect crops.
   - **Mission 5 (Sol 88) — Storm Over the Ice**: Modelled solar eruption warning from Earth; player coordinates moving the crew and rover behind reinforced water-jacket shelter walls.

3. **Debrief & Celebration**:
   - Reflection quiz, badge awards (*Oxygen Guardian, Solar Engineer, Ice Explorer, Greenhouse Guardian, Space Safety*), and free-roam exploration.

## NASA Data & Scientific Provenance
- **NASA NSSDC Mars Fact Sheet**: Surface gravity ($3.73\text{ m/s}^2$), solar constant ($586.2\text{ W/m}^2$), atmospheric pressure ($6.36\text{ mb}$), sol duration ($24\text{ h } 39\text{ min}$).
- **NASA Space Weather Database (DONKI)**: Real-time and archived Coronal Mass Ejection (CME) and Solar Energetic Particle (SEP) storm alerts.
- **Mars Acoustics & Perseverance Microphones (Maurice et al., Nature 2022)**: Speed of sound ($240\text{ m/s}$) and thin-gas high-frequency attenuation.
- **Locomotion Mechanics (Carr & McGee, PLoS ONE 2009)**: Froude number scaling for suited planetary walking ($0.863\text{ m/s}$) and loping ($2.65\text{ m/s}$).
- **Subsurface Ice Mapping (NASA SWIM Project)**: Mid-latitude buried glacial sheets.
